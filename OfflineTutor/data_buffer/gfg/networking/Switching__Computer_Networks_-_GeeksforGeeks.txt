# Switching | Computer Networks - GeeksforGeeks

Switching is the process of transferring data from one device to another within the same network or across different networks using switches. It mainly occurs at the Data Link Layer (Layer 2) of the OSI model, where data is forwarded in the form of frames based on MAC addresses.A switch connects multiple devices within a network.It reduces interference by sending data only to the intended device.It forwards data frames to the correct port using MAC addresses.It delivers data directly to connected devices (computers, phones).For communication outside the local network, it forwards data to a router.Example: A website request passes through multiple switches and routers before reaching the server.Network SwitchingNetwork Switching is a specific type of switching used in computer networks. It is about transferring data packets from one device (node) to another across a network.It involves the following steps:Frame Reception: The switch receives a data frame from a connected device.MAC Address Extraction: The switch reads the destination MAC address from the frame header.Lookup: The switch searches its MAC (switching) table for the destination address.Forwarding:If the address is found, the frame is sent to the corresponding port. If not found, the switch performs flooding (forwards the frame to all ports except the incoming port).Table Update: The switch learns the source MAC address and updates its MAC table for future use.Frame Transmission: The frame is delivered to the correct destination device or forwarded toward the network.Types of SwitchingThere are three types of switching methods:Types for SwitchingMessage Switching: Message switching is one of the earliest switching techniques. In this method:The entire message is sent to an intermediate node.The message is stored temporarily at that node before being forwarded to the next node.No dedicated path is established between the sender and receiver.Analogy: Like sending a physical letter that is first stored at a post office before moving to the next branch.Circuit Switching:Circuit switching is a connection-oriented method widely used in traditional telephone networks. In this method:a dedicated path is established between the sender and receiver before any data transmission beginsThe connection remains reserved for the entire duration of the communication sessionBoth ends enjoy a fixed bandwidth and low latency.Analogy: Like reserving a private road for the whole trip, even if no cars are on it for some time.Packet Switching:Packet switching is the foundation of modern computer networks, including the Internet. In this method:Data is divided into small packets.Each packet carries a portion of the message and the destination address.Packets may travel through different paths in the network.At the destination, packets are reassembled to reconstruct the original message.Analogy: Like sending a book in separate chapters through different courier routes, then reassembling them at the destination.Comparison of Switching TypesMessage SwitchingCircuit SwitchingPacket SwitchingNo dedicated path; the entire message is stored and forwarded at each node.A fixed, dedicated path is established before transmission.Data is split into packets that follow dynamic or virtual paths.Very inefficient due to full message storage and forwarding.Inefficient because bandwidth remains reserved even when idle.Highly efficient due to optimal bandwidth utilization.High transmission delay.Low delay after initial setup.Delay varies based on network conditions.Less reliable and obsolete.Reliable with guaranteed connection.Reliable with error control and retransmission.Used in telegraph systems.Used in traditional telephone networks.Used in the Internet.
